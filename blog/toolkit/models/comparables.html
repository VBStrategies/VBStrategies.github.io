Model · Relative valuation

Trading comparables

Values a company by what the market pays for similar listed companies, scaled by a common measure such as EBITDA or earnings.

Best forMature firms with many listed peers
Main inputsPeer prices, net debt, EBITDA, earnings, book value
EffortHours, not days

1 · The formula

Value of target = Peer multiple × Target's metric
for example: Enterprise value = median peer EV/EBITDA × target EBITDA

A multiple standardises price so companies of different size can be compared. There are two families, and they must never be mixed:

FamilyNumeratorPairs withExamples
Enterprise value multiplesEnterprise value (equity + net debt + leases + minorities)Metrics before interest, available to all capital providersEV/EBITDA, EV/EBIT, EV/Sales
Equity multiplesMarket capitalisationMetrics after interest, available to shareholders onlyP/E, P/B, dividend yield

Mixing them, for example dividing enterprise value by net income, gives a number that means nothing: the numerator includes debt holders, the denominator does not.

2 · Step by step

  1. Choose 5–10 peers. Same business model first, then similar growth, risk and size. Geography matters less than business model: a European and a US maker of the same product are often better peers than two European firms in different businesses.
  2. Choose the multiple that fits the business (table below).
  3. Compute every peer's multiple on the same basis. Same period (trailing twelve months or next year's forecast), same accounting treatment, same currency.
  4. Describe the distribution. Use the median, not the average, and look at the range. One extreme value can move an average a lot and a median hardly at all.
  5. Explain the differences. A peer with a higher multiple usually has higher growth, lower risk or higher margins. If the target differs on those, its multiple should differ too.
  6. Apply to the target and bridge to equity. Enterprise value minus net debt, leases and minorities gives equity value; divide by diluted shares for a value per share.

Which multiple for which business

MultipleUse forDriven mainly by
EV/EBITDAIndustrials, defence, most capital-intensive firmsReinvestment needs, tax rate, risk, growth
EV/EBITFirms with very different depreciationAs above, after the cost of replacing assets
P/EStable, profitable firms with similar debtGrowth, payout ratio, risk
P/BBanks and insurersReturn on equity versus cost of equity
EV/SalesLoss-making or early-stage firmsOperating margin the firm can reach
Every multiple hides a discounted cash flow model. The third column lists its "companion variable": a peer can only deserve a higher multiple if it is better on that driver. This is why comparables and DCF are two views of the same thing, not rival methods.

3 · When to use it, and when it misleads

Use whenSeveral genuinely comparable listed peers exist; you need a market check on a DCF; you need a quick first estimate before a full model.
Misleads whenThe whole sector is over- or undervalued (you inherit the market's mistake); peers differ in growth, leverage or accounting; the target has no real peers.

European pitfalls to watch: since IFRS 16, leases are debt and EBITDA excludes lease costs, so peers reporting under US GAAP are not directly comparable; companies report in different currencies and some calendarise their financial year differently.

4 · Worked example

A fictional industrial company, Nordwerk AG, with EBITDA of €400m, net income of €220m, net debt of €600m and 100m diluted shares. Six listed peers (fictional) trade at:

PeerEV/EBITDAP/ENote
Peer A8.5x13.0xSlower growth
Peer B9.2x14.5x
Peer C10.1x15.0x
Peer D10.8x16.0x
Peer E11.5x17.5xHigher margins
Peer F14.0x24.0xTakeover speculation: outlier
Median10.45x15.5xAverage would be 10.7x and 16.7x

EV/EBITDA route. 10.45 × €400m = €4,180m enterprise value. Minus €600m net debt = €3,580m equity value, or €35.80 per share. Using the interquartile range of peer multiples (9.4x–11.3x) gives €31.70–39.30 per share.

P/E cross-check. 15.5 × €220m = €3,410m equity value, or €34.10 per share. Close to the EV/EBITDA result, which is reassuring; a large gap would point to a difference in debt or tax between Nordwerk and its peers.

Result: a range of roughly €32–39 per share, centred around €35. Note how Peer F would pull an average up: the median ignores it, which is exactly why it is used.

5 · Where it fits in the strategy

In VB Europe Strategic, comparables are the standard cross-check for every company, never the only method:

  • Security and defence, industry and infrastructure, energy: EV/EBITDA against European peers, alongside a DCF.
  • Technology and chips: EV/EBIT or P/E, with care for very different growth rates.
  • Health and life sciences: P/E and EV/EBITDA for established firms; pipelines need rNPV.
  • Private equity sleeve: price to net asset value against other listed private equity firms.
